We Chased an Eclipse and It Reminded Us Why Your Business Can't Miss a Single Call

When the Moon Meets the Sun

Last week, our entire Omnidesq team drove three hours north to watch something we'd been planning for months: a total solar eclipse right at sunset. We set up our cameras on a bluff overlooking Lake Michigan, and as the moon slowly covered the sun, the sky turned this incredible orange-purple that I've never seen before. We captured the whole thing on video, and honestly, watching it back still gives me chills.

But here's the thing that struck me as we stood there in the sudden twilight at 6 PM. We had one shot at this. The eclipse lasted exactly four minutes and twelve seconds. If we'd been late, if we'd missed our window, if we'd been stuck in traffic or forgotten our equipment, that would have been it. No do-overs. No second chances. And it got me thinking about something that happens in small businesses every single day.

The Calls You Never Know You Missed

Every day, dental offices and small businesses across Milwaukee miss calls. A patient calls at 5:15 PM when you've already left. Someone dials during lunch when the front desk is empty. Three people call at once and two go to voicemail. According to our data, the average dental practice misses about 30% of incoming calls during business hours. That's nearly one in three potential patients who never get through.

Just like that eclipse, these moments don't come back around. That person calling about tooth pain? They're probably calling the next dentist on their list right now. The business owner looking for services? They've already moved on. In the time it takes for you to check voicemail and call back, they've booked with someone else. Missed calls aren't just inconvenient. They're revenue walking out the door, except you never even knew it was there.
